How to Play the Online Piano

My Piano provides multiple intuitive input methods designed to give you a responsive, realistic playing experience whether on a desktop, laptop, tablet, or phone.

🖱️

Mouse & Touch Controls

Click any white or black key to strike that note. You can also press and drag your mouse across keys for a smooth glissando. On touchscreens, multi-touch chord playing is fully supported.

⌨️

Computer Keyboard Play

Use your desktop or laptop keyboard to play naturally. White keys map to your home row (A, S, D, F, G, H, J), while black keys map to upper keys (W, E, T, Y, U).

Changing Octaves

Jump to any octave using the quick pills (Octave 1 to 7), drag the top mini-piano seekbar, or tap the [ and ] bracket keys on your computer keyboard to shift up or down.

Fine-tune audio levels with the volume slider or click the speaker icon to mute/unmute instantly. The Web Audio API ensures pure acoustics without audio lag or clipping.

Piano Keyboard Guide

Whether you are an aspiring pianist or brushing up on music theory, understanding how a piano is arranged makes learning songs faster and more enjoyable.

Understanding White Keys (Natural Notes)

The white keys represent the seven natural notes in Western music: A, B, C, D, E, F, and G. This seven-note sequence repeats continuously across the keyboard from left to right. As you move to the right, the pitch becomes higher; moving left produces deeper bass tones.

Understanding Black Keys (Sharps & Accidentals)

Black keys are arranged in repeating groups of two and three. They represent accidentals—sharps (♯) and flats (♭). In My Piano, these keys produce A#, C#, D#, F#, and G# notes, positioned precisely between their corresponding natural keys.

Finding Middle C (C4)

Middle C (labeled C4 on the piano keyboard) is the most important anchor note for beginners. It sits directly to the left of any group of two black keys near the center of the instrument. Octave 4 on My Piano is selected by default for easy song practice.

Octaves & Musical Pitch

An octave is the interval between one musical pitch and another with double its frequency. On our 7-octave piano, moving from C1 up to C7 demonstrates how the fundamental pitch doubles every octave while preserving the same harmonic note name.

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct, verified answers to common questions about playing piano online with My Piano.

How do I play piano online for free?

Simply visit My Piano in any browser on your computer, tablet, or phone. You can immediately strike keys using your mouse, tap with your fingers on touchscreen devices, or type on your computer keyboard. There are no fees, trials, or downloads required.

Can I play the piano using my computer keyboard?

Yes. Home row keys (A through J) trigger natural white keys, while upper keys (W, E, T, Y, U, O, P) trigger black sharp keys in the selected octave. You can toggle key badges on the piano by clicking the "Keys" button in the control toolbar.

Can I use this online piano on my phone or tablet?

Yes. My Piano is fully responsive and supports multi-touch gestures on mobile devices. You can play multiple notes at once or slide your finger across keys for glissando. Rotating your device to landscape provides a wider, more comfortable layout.

How do I change octaves or navigate the keyboard?

You can jump between octaves using three methods: clicking the Octave 1 through 7 pills in the toolbar, dragging the miniature piano seekbar navigator at the top, or using the [ (octave down) and ] (octave up) keyboard shortcuts.

Is any installation, plugin, or account required?

No installation is required. My Piano is a pure client-side web application running on modern web standards (HTML5 and Web Audio API). It operates without Flash, plugins, app downloads, or user registration.

Why is there no sound when I first open the website?

All modern browsers (Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox) have autoplay security policies that mute audio until you interact with the page. Sound activates immediately as soon as you click or tap your first piano key.

Can I play multiple notes or chords at the same time?

Yes. My Piano features a polyphonic audio architecture that handles simultaneous notes smoothly, allowing you to play full triads, chords, melodies, and basslines simultaneously.
